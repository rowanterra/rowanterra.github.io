I successfully defended my Ph.D. in Biological Sciences at <a href="https://www.duq.edu/academics/colleges-and-schools/science-and-engineering/academics/departments-and-programs/biological-sciences/index.php">Duquesne University</a> in October 2026 (degree conferral pending). My doctoral research on biogeochemical processes in abandoned mine drainage (AMD) was carried out at Duquesne and <a href="https://edx.netl.doe.gov/sites/geomicrobiology/">NETL-DOE</a> under an <a href="https://orise.orau.gov/index.html">ORISE</a> research fellowship. These projects (1) characterized AMD feedstocks using field and computational methods and (2) explored microbiological mining for critical mineral (CMM) extraction and recovery. We are now preparing the remaining manuscripts from this work for publication.

I have a decade of research experience spanning biological, chemical, ecological, and computational sciences. These opportunities have helped me flesh out my preference for multidisciplinary approaches to scientific questions. More information can be found in my CV.

*In November 2026 I tentatively begin as an ORISE postdoctoral research fellow with Carnegie Mellon University and NETL.* I am open to longer-term tenure-track and research and development (R&D) positions in academic, industrial, and government settings, with a preference for multidisciplinary laboratories that mesh bench and computational work. I also continue my consulting work through Terra Cognita LLC. Ultimately, I would like to work toward becoming a principal investigator in any of these environments.
